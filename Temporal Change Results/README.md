# Temporal Change Detection and Sand Area Analysis

This analysis compares refined sand masks for 25 study locations
around the Abqaiq–Salasil Highway across eight years, 2019–2026.

## Notebook and Inputs

The implementation is in `Temporal_Change_Detection.ipynb`
at the repository root.

Inputs:
- `Texture Morphology Results/masks/<year>/*_refined.tif`
- `RawDataset/metadata.csv`
- Processed multispectral GeoTIFFs for RGB comparison figures
- `Texture Morphology Results/summary.csv` for area consistency checks

The input masks are generated by BSI–Otsu segmentation, followed
by texture filtering, a SWIR-based exclusion rule, and morphological
refinement in `texture_morphology.py`.

Input mask values:
- 0: classified non-sand
- 1: classified sand
- 255: invalid, excluded from analysis

## Method

Annual sand area and coverage are calculated for all 200 observations.
Each 10 m × 10 m pixel represents 100 square metres, or 0.0001 km².

Temporal comparisons include:
- 175 consecutive-year comparisons: seven intervals at 25 sites
- 25 full-period comparisons: 2019 versus 2026

Only pixels valid at both dates are compared.

Change-map values:
- 0: persistent non-sand
- 1: persistent sand
- 2: sand gain
- 3: sand loss
- 255: invalid or excluded

Calculations:
- Sand area = sand-pixel count × pixel area
- Annual sand coverage (%) = sand pixels / valid pixels × 100
- Net change = gain area − loss area
- Total changed area = gain area + loss area
- Changed area (%) = total changed area / common valid area × 100
- Relative sand-area change (%) = (later sand area − earlier sand area)
  / earlier sand area × 100

Pixel area is calculated from the GeoTIFF affine transform and
checked against the expected value of 0.0001 km².

For temporal comparisons, earlier and later sand areas are measured
over the same common valid pixels. Relative sand-area change is
undefined when the earlier sand area is zero.

## Results Archive

Download and extract `Temporal_Change_Results.zip`.

It contains:
- `tables/annual_sand_area.csv`: 200 annual observation records
- `tables/temporal_change_statistics.csv`: 200 comparison records
- `tables/change_2019_2026.csv`: 25 full-period comparison records
- `change_maps/`: 200 georeferenced classified change maps
- `figures/`: five figures
- `analysis_provenance.json`: input version and interpretation notes

The five figures are:
- Annual coverage trends for road-centred sites L1–L6, alongside
  a coverage heatmap for surrounding sites L7–L25
- Net classified sand-area change from 2019 to 2026 for all 25 sites
- RGB imagery and change-map comparisons for L1, L7, and L17

L1, L7, and L17 are selected examples. They are not presented
as the sites with the largest changes.

## Checks

The notebook checks:
- Expected observation and location counts
- Availability of all eight years for every site
- Duplicate site/year records
- Allowed mask values and valid-pixel availability
- Image dimensions and coordinate reference systems
- Matching spatial grids across years for each site
- Agreement with `refined_area_km2` in the rounded refinement summary
- Consistency of change-class counts with common valid area
- Equality between net sand-area change and gain minus loss

These checks establish computational consistency, not segmentation
accuracy.

## Interpretation and Limitations

Outputs represent changes in classified sand coverage.

Segmentation errors, differences in illumination and reflectance,
and boundary processing can contribute to apparent changes.
The temporal-analysis notebook does not apply additional
radiometric normalization.

Visual comparisons provide qualitative checks, not independent
ground-truth validation. Some non-sand surfaces may be confused
with sand and should be examined during segmentation evaluation.

The imagery has a 10 m output grid; the SWIR bands have native
20 m resolution. One observation per year cannot capture
changes between acquisition dates.

Sand-covered area within a study site does not directly measure
sand covering the highway. Six sites are road-centred, while
the remaining sites cover surrounding terrain.

Results do not independently establish dune migration rates,
road-lane obstruction, or the causes of observed changes.
Interpretation remains subject to segmentation evaluation.

These updated outputs replace the earlier temporal results
generated from the previous spectral–texture clustering masks.
